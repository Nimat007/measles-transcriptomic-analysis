# 05 — Cross-Dataset Convergence Analysis

## 1. Overview

This work package investigates whether transcriptional signals identified in bulk RNA-seq analysis converge with findings from the single-cell RNA-seq and pseudobulk analyses in the Measles Virus (MeV) host-response thesis.

The analysis integrates evidence across datasets and analytical resolutions to identify overlapping gene-level signals and lineage-associated patterns. It also includes exploratory summaries and sensitivity analyses to document how the observed convergence depends on the selected cell populations and available lineage support.

The objective is to assess consistency between complementary transcriptomic analyses, not to assume that signals observed in different datasets are automatically equivalent or independently validated.

## 2. Datasets and Inputs

- **Bulk RNA-seq:** GSE229685, used for bulk differential-expression discovery.
- **Single-cell RNA-seq:** GSE168775, used for cell-level characterization, annotation, and pseudobulk analysis.
- **Bulk differential-expression results:** Outputs generated by the bulk discovery workflow.
- **Pseudobulk differential-expression results:** Primary broad-lineage and secondary cluster-level results generated by the pseudobulk workflow.
- **Gene-identifier harmonization outputs:** Mapping and audit tables used to support comparison between gene identifiers and symbols.

The analysis depends on compatible gene identifiers, clearly defined comparison groups, and the differential-expression outputs generated by the preceding work packages.

## 3. Main Analysis Script

`08_Cross_Dataset.R`

This script performs the cross-dataset comparison and generates gene-overlap summaries, convergence tables, exploratory cluster summaries, and lineage-support sensitivity outputs.

## 4. Analysis Workflow

### Step 1 — Prepare and harmonize gene identifiers

Bulk and single-cell-derived differential-expression results must use compatible gene identifiers before overlap can be assessed.

The preceding identifier-harmonization workflow provides mapping metrics and audit tables to document the conversion between reference identifiers and gene symbols.

Identifier mapping is an important source of uncertainty: unmapped genes, duplicated mappings, or inconsistent identifiers can affect the number of genes considered shared between datasets.

### Step 2 — Compare bulk and pseudobulk findings

The analysis compares the bulk differential-expression results with the primary broad-lineage pseudobulk findings.

It summarizes shared genes and other gene-level evidence across the two analytical approaches, allowing the results to be examined for cross-dataset consistency.

Because bulk RNA-seq measures a mixture of cells while pseudobulk analysis represents selected annotated populations, agreement or disagreement may reflect differences in cellular composition, biological context, statistical power, or analysis design.

### Step 3 — Evaluate gene-level convergence

Gene-level convergence tables preserve the evidence used to compare bulk and single-cell-derived results.

These outputs support follow-up examination of individual genes and help distinguish simple overlap from more detailed agreement in the direction or strength of the observed transcriptional signals, where those fields are available.

The presence of a gene in both result sets does not, by itself, establish that its effect direction is concordant or that the gene is a validated biomarker.

### Step 4 — Examine multi-lineage patterns

The workflow includes summaries that investigate whether signals are observed across multiple broad lineages.

These comparisons help distinguish genes associated with a particular lineage from signals that appear across several analyzed populations.

Lineage-level recurrence should be interpreted in light of the number of eligible populations, the underlying sample structure, and the rules used to define significant or overlapping genes.

### Step 5 — Summarize exploratory cluster-level findings

Exploratory cluster summaries provide a more detailed view of the relationship between bulk findings and selected single-cell populations.

These outputs are intended to support hypothesis generation and prioritization for further analysis. Cluster-level overlap alone should not be interpreted as proof of cell-type-specific causality.

### Step 6 — Perform lineage-support sensitivity analysis

The analysis includes a lineage-support sensitivity report to document how the available lineage evidence affects the cross-dataset comparison.

This is important because a conclusion based on a small number of eligible lineages may be less robust than a pattern supported across several adequately represented populations.

The sensitivity analysis provides context for interpreting the breadth and stability of the observed convergence.

### Step 7 — Generate summary tables and visualizations

The workflow produces tables summarizing convergence at different levels of resolution, including gene-level comparisons, lineage-associated patterns, and exploratory cluster summaries.

A convergence visualization is also generated to provide an overview of the relationship between bulk and single-cell-derived results.

Figures and tables should be interpreted alongside their underlying result files, identifier-mapping records, and analysis criteria.
